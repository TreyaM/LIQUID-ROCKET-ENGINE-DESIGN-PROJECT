# How do you power a liquid rocket engine?





## BASICS: 



* you wanna throw stuff out the back as quickly as possible, that increases the exhaust gas velocity and thus increases the thrust produced. it follows Newtons third law: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
* Pressure + Heat > Kinetic Energy which means that the hot subsonic high pressure gas is converted into cooler supersonic high velocity gas. by keeping the pressure and temperature as high as possible in the combustion chamber while managing heat. the heat is the energy source that can be thought of as converting to work to move the vehicle. 
* all the heat stored in the system is known as enthalpy. higher the enthalpy, more potential for useful work to be done.
* the important thing is that **pressure flows through a gradient.**
* thus we have a pressure budget: how much pressure needs to be created and sustained for the fluids to be moving in the right direction?
* Hence we are always limited by the pressure present upstream



## TYPES of ENGINES:

* Cold Gas Thrusters
* Monoprop
* Biprop



## TYPES of FEED SYSTEMS:

* Pressure Fed: heavy pressurant tanks
* Pump Fed: lighter tanks and denser propellants, but energy required for the pumps to work





## TYPES of CYCLES:



### Open Cycle

* a gas generator is used to run the turbine and pumps and its exhaust gas is fed into the atmosphere. the fuel is tapped off from the main fuel line and fed into the gas generator, but the downside is the formation of soot and the fact that the unburnt fuel is lost as exhaust



### Closed Cycle: OX Rich

* here gas generator is referred to as the pre burner
* all of the oxidiser is sent into the pre burner along with a fraction of the fuel. since only a little fuel is used in the pre burner, the ox still retains its chemical energy for a full combustion process in the main chamber. 
* the pressure in the turbine is kept to a minimum so if thats connected to the main combustion chamber then the gas would want to flow backwards through the system. hence, the pressure in the preburner is kept extremely high by using two pump for the fuel: one low pressure pump for the chamber, and one high pressure pump for the pre burner

### 

### Closed Cycle: Fuel Rich

* here all of the fuel is sent into the preburner with a fraction of the ox. coking and soot is the main problem but is eliminated by using LH2, which is lightweight but requires very large pumps to pressure it.
* we use two difference pre burners for each pump, both fuel rich. but there is a big issue. LOX + hot gaseous hydrogen connected by the same shaft is a big problem because if if the gas flows through the shaft and into the lox tank, its game over. so nasa developed a purge seal by having an even higher pressured inert helium in the middle such that if anything leaks, it'll flow into the pump and not back into the tank. 

### 

### Full Flow Stages Combustion Cycle

* PB 1 is Fuel rich with a fraction of ox flowing
* PB 2 is Ox rich with a fraction of fuel flowing
* we couple the respective turbines and pumps. 
* the biggest advantage is the super high temperature in the pre burner and also the fact that the work needed to run the pumps is split between the two PBS.
* The temperature drop through the turbine for a given amount of shaft work is the least for full flow staged compared to ox and fuel rich because it has the highest mass flow rates. this allows the high temperature of the pre burners to be sustained by the gases into the combustion chamber.
